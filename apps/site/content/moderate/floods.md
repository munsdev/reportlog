---
title: Floods
description: What to do when a site is flooded with reports, and the tools that make it visible and stoppable without hiding anything.
---

A site that matters to somebody will at some point be flooded: the same report filed hundreds of times, or a script trying its forms. The **Flood screen**, under **Moderation**, is for moderators and administrators.

## Seeing it

- **Reports per hour** over the last day.
- **Refusals per hour** at the forms' checks, by which check turned them away. See [what every form checks](/docs/admin/doors-and-intake/#what-every-form-checks).
- **Identical answer sets**: reports whose answers are exactly the same, grouped, with how many there are. The same view per organization, for accounts filed through one.

## Stopping it

| Tool                   | What it does                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pause intake**       | Stops new reports on the whole site, with a notice you write telling people when to come back. Corrections and everything already filed are untouched. |
| **Harder puzzle**      | Makes the puzzle every form solves in the background harder, so each send costs a script more. It costs a person a little more waiting too.            |
| **Decline in bulk**    | Declines a group of identical reports at once, with one reason. No email is sent to their reporters.                                                   |
| **Withhold tip links** | Withholds the tip links sent in a window of time, from the journalist desk.                                                                            |

Each asks for your second factor again, and each use is recorded. **Pausing intake and the harder puzzle are shown publicly** on the board's activity page, with when they were used, because a site that quietly stopped taking reports would look the same as one nobody used. Declined reports are counted publicly by reason.

Nothing here deletes anything. A declined report stays sealed in its chain; it is not published.
